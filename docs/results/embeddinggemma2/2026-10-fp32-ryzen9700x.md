# EmbeddingGemma 2: FP32 vs BF16 precision on a Ryzen 7 9700X VM

Host: 4 physical Zen 5 cores (AVX512_CORE_BF16, no AMX), ~15 GiB. **Not
Turin**, but the same Zen 5 core. Measured on 2026-10-23 with
`bench/matrix.py --precision bf16|fp32 --threads 4,2,1`, with one binary for
both precisions.

`b16-s8192` and `mix-b16-long` (131k live tokens) are left out because they
get OOM-killed on this 16 GiB host in either precision.

## Accuracy

Both precisions are checked against the PyTorch FP32 fixtures
(`fixtures/parity.json`).

| Precision | Min cosine vs PyTorch FP32 | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| `bf16` (default) | ≥ 0.999 | 0.999 |
| `fp32` | 0.99999994 | 0.99999 |

## Speed and memory

Geomean FP32/BF16 p50 over all 40 cells:

| Threads | Geomean | Best cell | Worst cell |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 1.41x | 0.80x (b1-s7) | 1.65x |
| 2 | 1.61x | 0.90x (b1-s7) | 1.79x |
| 1 | 1.77x | 1.22x (b1-s7) | 1.88x |

- p95 tracks p50 in both precisions.
- RSS at load is 324 MiB in both precisions: FP32 reads the BF16-stored
  weights through oneDNN's f32:bf16 brgemm.
- Peak RSS is lower in FP32. FP32 makes no BF16 activation copies and
  projects after pooling. For example, b1-s4096 at t4 peaks at 1267 MiB
  in FP32 and 1756 MiB in BF16.

process high-water mark, so a cell's value includes the larger cells that

FP32 is about 1.8x slower at one thread. That is the Zen 5 compute gap:
BF16 dot products (`vdpbf16ps`) do twice the work per instruction of an FP32
FMA. With more threads, BF16 loses part of its lead on short inputs to
activation conversion and parallel-region overhead.

## FP32 with f32-packed weights (not adopted)

As an experiment, the same FP32 graph was run with weights widened to f32 at
pack time. Two interleaved rounds:

| | t4 | t1 |
|---|---|---|
| Geomean p50 vs BF16-stored | 1.04-1.05x | 1.04x |
| Short inputs (b1-s7, b1-s19) | 1.24-1.43x slower | 1.16-1.50x slower |
| ≥ 512 tokens | 0-4% faster | 2% faster |
| RSS at load | 585-662 MiB (vs 325) | 585 MiB (vs 324) |

Short inputs are bound by weight bandwidth, so reading twice the bytes costs
more than the in-kernel upconversion saves. FP32 therefore keeps BF16
storage.
